## Lens Conclusion

The best Bosque del Apache lens plan is a compact system kit, not a single maximum-reach answer.

- **Sony kit:** use the **Sony FE 200-600mm F5.6-6.3 G OSS** as the primary long wildlife lens, keep the **Sony 24-105mm f/4** ready for blast-offs, sky, landscape, reflection, and habitat frames, use the **Sony 100-400mm F4.5-5.6 GM OSS** when lighter close-pass/flight handling matters, and use the **Sony 400-800mm** when birds are distant and light is good.
- **Canon:** use the **Canon RF 100-500mm** as the primary wildlife lens. Keep the **Canon 24-105mm** ready for wide/context frames. Use the **Canon RF 200-800mm** when birds are distant and light is good.

The practical field rule: start with flexible zooms for dawn, dusk, and moving birds; add reach later when subjects are distant, static, and better lit.

A 70-200mm f/2.8 is worth considering only as an optional fourth lens for close birds, compression, portraits, travel details, and low-light static subjects. It should not replace the 24-105mm or the primary wildlife zoom.

For the shared **Bosque + Tanzania** kit, the 70-200mm f/2.8 moves up in priority. Tanzania vehicle-based mammal work rewards close-to-mid telephoto flexibility, so the four-lens shared kit keeps 24-105mm + 70-200mm f/2.8 + 100-400mm/100-500mm + 400-800mm/200-800mm plus a 1.4x teleconverter. If Tanzania travel weight forces a three-lens cut, drop the super-reach lens before dropping the 70-200mm.

## Articles And Guides

### 2026-04-19 - Evie Wilder, "Sandhill Crane Migration"

- Link: https://eviewilder.com/blog/sandhill-crane-migration-bosque-del-apache-winter/
- Type: Trip report, sandhill crane behavior, winter field preparation.
- Summary: A recent field story centered on sandhill cranes in severe winter conditions. The useful planning value is the concrete advice about cold, wind, snow, condensation, batteries, and being set up before first light.
- Lens / camera takeaway: Recommends a 70-600mm lens range, tripod/monopod support, extra batteries, continuous autofocus, burst mode, and shutter speeds of 1/1600 or faster for freezing flight.
- Apply at Bosque: Treat early mornings as a physical endurance problem as much as a camera problem. Have gloves, batteries, rain/snow protection, and the long lens ready before the first crane movement.

### 2025-11-01 - The Quite Wild, "15 Essential Tips for Bird Photography for Beginners at Bosque del Apache"

- Link: https://thequitewild.com/bird-photography-for-beginners-bosque-del-apache/
- Type: Beginner instruction and settings guide.
- Summary: A structured beginner guide covering season, locations, camera settings, autofocus, exposure, telephoto options, support, and field tactics.
- Lens / camera takeaway: Recommends value wildlife zooms such as 100-400mm, 150-600mm, and 200-600mm; notes that APS-C bodies make 100-400mm feel like roughly 150-600mm; says 1.4x teleconverters can help if autofocus remains reliable.
- Apply at Bosque: Good checklist for pre-trip practice. Use the article's settings as starting points, then tune for your camera body and the actual light.

### 2024-06-03 updated - Outdoor Photographer, "Extreme Close-Up"

- Link: https://www.outdoorphotographer.com/tips-techniques/wildlife-techniques/extreme-close-up/
- Type: Wildlife technique article with Bosque example.
- Summary: A long-lens technique piece using Bosque as a prime example for birds in flight. It is more about extreme focal lengths and support discipline than general travel planning.
- Lens / camera takeaway: The Bosque snow goose example uses a Canon EOS-1D X, EF 400mm f/2.8L II, and EF 2x III for 800mm, with gimbal/tripod support.
- Apply at Bosque: Extreme reach can produce tight flight images, but this is specialized. Do not let the 800mm example override the need for a flexible zoom and a wide lens.

### 2024-04-21 updated, 2023-11-22 published - WildernessShots, "How to Shoot Wildlife Photography"

- Link: https://www.wildernessshots.com/how-to-shoot-wildlife-photography
- Type: General wildlife technique guide with Bosque examples.
- Summary: A broad wildlife tutorial that covers long lenses, teleconverters, support, behavior, action, composition, light, shutter speed, panning, and focus technique. It includes Bosque examples for snow geese and cranes.
- Lens / camera takeaway: Explains why telephoto zooms are often more practical than primes, why teleconverters cost light and image quality, and why zooming out matters when action gets close.
- Apply at Bosque: This supports using a flexible long zoom first: Sony 200-600mm or Sony 100-400mm f/4.5-5.6 depending distance and handling, and Canon RF 100-500mm. It also supports saving teleconverters for bright, distant, static subjects.

### 2022-12 - NANPA, "Bosque del Apache National Wildlife Refuge Regional Event"

- Link: https://nanpa.org/eventcalendar/bosque-del-apache-national-wildlife-refuge-regional-event-december-4-7-2022/
- Type: Photo-tour planning and gear guidance.
- Summary: A practical event page that identifies likely photo locations, expected car-based shooting, weather constraints, and a gear range for birds and scenery.
- Lens / camera takeaway: Recommends telephoto range from 100-400mm upward, 400mm or larger when possible, optional teleconverters, and a sturdy tripod with ball or gimbal head.
- Apply at Bosque: Bring both wide and long options. Most shooting is from vehicles or short walks, so the kit can include a long lens and support without requiring a strenuous hike.

### 2021-07/08 - BirdWatching / Rick Sammon, "The Best Camera Settings for Bird Photography"

- Link: https://www.birdwatchingdaily.com/photography/how-to-photograph-birds/best-camera-settings-for-bird-photography/
- Type: Bird photography settings and gear article.
- Summary: A practical settings article on shutter speed, autofocus, exposure, frame rate, and mood. It includes a Bosque sunrise photo example.
- Lens / camera takeaway: Names the Canon RF 100-500mm as a go-to bird/wildlife zoom and describes adding a 1.4x teleconverter for 700mm. The Bosque sunrise example uses a Canon EOS 5D Mark III with a 24-105mm lens.
- Apply at Bosque: Carry the 24-105mm. Wide and mid-range frames are not backup shots; they are part of the Bosque story.

### 2019-11-03 URL date / visible page shows Jan 13 - Chasing Hippoz, "My Photography & Travel Guide to Bosque Del Apache, NM"

- Link: https://www.chasinghippoz.com/travel-photo-blog/2019/11/3/wxk15i7zdi06279j8bzassi9cgs98a
- Type: Travel and photography guide.
- Summary: A practical visitor guide covering when to go, where to stay, where to shoot, light, backgrounds, clothing, and gear.
- Lens / camera takeaway: Recommends 500mm, 600mm, or 800mm for isolating cranes and geese, but specifically says the Canon RF 100-500mm is a strong flexible option and that 70-200mm or 100-400mm can be very useful when birds lift off in big groups.
- Apply at Bosque: Use this as support for a two-body strategy: long zoom for birds, 24-105mm or 70-200mm-class lens for scale and environment.

### 2019-07-02 - Outdoor Photographer / OEL, "Bosque del Apache National Wildlife Refuge"

- Link: https://www.oelmag.com/bosque-del-apache-national-wildlife-refuge/
- Type: Location feature.
- Summary: A concise Bosque overview from Dawn Wilson covering winter subjects, weather, mist, low-angle perspective, panning, slow-shutter flock work, and environmental composition.
- Lens / camera takeaway: No specific lens prescription, but it reinforces the need for both bird action and environmental frames with mountains, sky, and low perspective.
- Apply at Bosque: Do not only chase full-frame birds. Work low when safe and legal, isolate birds against cleaner backgrounds, and photograph flocks in their setting.

### 2016-12-21 - LifePixel / Bob Coates, "Bosque del Apache Wildlife Refuge"

- Link: https://www.lifepixel.com/photography-locations/bosque-del-apache-wildlife-refuge
- Type: Field technique and gear experience.
- Summary: A field-based guide focused on sandhill cranes, flight tracking, support, asking for local intel, and anticipating behavior.
- Lens / camera takeaway: Uses a Panasonic Lumix GX85 with a 100-400mm lens, equivalent to an 800mm full-frame field of view, while noting that gimbal support is often useful for flight tracking.
- Apply at Bosque: Reach is helpful, but the strongest lesson is behavior reading. Watch posture and repeated flight paths so the lens is already pointed where action will happen.

### 2009-01-13 - Luminous Landscape, "Bosque del Apache"

- Link: https://luminous-landscape.com/bosque-del-apache/
- Type: Classic Bosque photo essay and gear note.
- Summary: A classic early-digital Bosque article emphasizing focal length, migratory bird abundance, and the usefulness of noting effective focal length on crop-sensor bodies.
- Lens / camera takeaway: Most images were made with a Canon 400mm f/4 DO IS on a Canon D30, giving a 640mm effective field of view.
- Apply at Bosque: The effective sweet spot for many bird images sits around 400-600mm full-frame equivalent or a little longer. This supports the Sony 200-600mm as a core long zoom, the 100-400mm f/4.5-5.6 as a lighter handling option, the 400-800mm as a reach specialist, and the Canon RF 100-500mm plan.

### 2008-07-15 - Cornell All About Birds / Marie Read, "Bosque Del Apache, New Mexico: A Bird Photographer's Playground"

- Link: https://www.allaboutbirds.org/news/bosque-del-apache-new-mexico-a-bird-photographers-playground/
- Type: Bosque-specific photography guide.
- Summary: One of the most useful Bosque articles for fieldcraft: Main Pool and Old Highway 1 locations, flight cues, wind/light alignment, manual exposure, panning, slow-shutter creativity, and varied focal lengths.
- Lens / camera takeaway: Says 300mm or 400mm can be enough for geese and cranes in flight, 500mm or 600mm often benefits from tripod/gimbal support, and short telephotos or wide-angle lenses work for flocks and habitat.
- Apply at Bosque: This is the strongest support for not defaulting to maximum reach. Start wider for blast-offs, then move to the long zoom as birds separate.

### 2008 original / maintained current site - Thom Hogan, "Bosque del Apache"

- Link: https://www.bythom.com/photographic-travel/united-states/bosque-del-apache.html
- Type: Gear discipline and field-position essay.
- Summary: A thoughtful counterpoint to gear obsession. Hogan describes using a D90 with 70-300mm and argues that prediction, positioning, and knowing what image you are trying to make can matter more than big exotic glass.
- Lens / camera takeaway: Notes he would bring a high-end body and 400mm f/2.8 TC today for serious work, but says a 600mm f/4 was too much for the moon/bird alignment images he was making.
- Apply at Bosque: Decide the shot first. If the goal is bird plus moon, bird plus sky, or flock shape, too much focal length can hurt.
