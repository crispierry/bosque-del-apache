# Lenses And Field Setup

## Status

Updated June 5, 2026. The final camera system is not confirmed, and the user wants to keep both Sony and Canon options for now. This file keeps Sony and Canon as separate branches while correcting the Sony recommendation: the Sony FE 200-600mm F5.6-6.3 G OSS is a primary wildlife recommendation, not a fallback, especially for Tanzania.

## Best Lens Decision

### Sony

The previous draft underweighted the **Sony FE 200-600mm F5.6-6.3 G OSS**. It should be treated as the **primary Sony long wildlife lens** for Bosque and the clearest Sony safari recommendation for Tanzania. The **Sony FE 100-400mm F4.5-5.6 GM OSS** remains valuable as the lighter handheld close-pass/flight option, and the **Sony FE 400-800mm F6.3-8 G OSS** becomes the specialized bright-light reach lens.

The best Sony kit is:

1. **Sony FE 24-105mm F4 G OSS** - wide flock scale, sky, reflections, mountains, weather, and environmental story frames.
2. **Sony FE 200-600mm F5.6-6.3 G OSS** - primary long wildlife lens for cranes, geese, distant ducks, and Tanzania wildlife.
3. **Sony FE 100-400mm F4.5-5.6 GM OSS** - lighter dawn/flight/close-pass lens when acquisition speed matters more than maximum reach.
4. **Sony FE 400-800mm F6.3-8 G OSS** - distant birds, fields, raptors, and deck work once light is good.
5. **Sony 1.4x and 2x teleconverters** - optional reach tools, not default pre-sunrise setups.

Do **not** leave the Sony 200-600mm out by default. If weight forces a Bosque cut, choose between 100-400 and 400-800 based on whether the priority is close flight handling or maximum bright-light reach. If weight forces a Tanzania cut, keep the 200-600 before the 400-800 unless distant birds are the dominant goal.

Teleconverter rules:

- Sony 100-400mm f/4.5-5.6 + 1.4x gives a useful 140-560mm f/6.3-8 range, but use it after light improves.
- Sony 400-800mm + 1.4x gives 560-1120mm f/9-11; reserve that for bright, distant, static subjects.
- Sony 200-600mm + 1.4x gives 280-840mm f/8-9; useful for record shots, not first-light flight.
- Sony 2x teleconverter options cost two stops. Treat them as bright-light, supported, distant-subject tools after autofocus and heat shimmer have been checked.

### Canon

Primary wildlife lens: **Canon RF 100-500mm**.

Why:

- It is the best Canon match for Bosque's mix of flight, sudden distance changes, vehicle shooting, and low-light dawn/dusk work.
- It aligns with published Bosque recommendations that value 100-500mm or 200-600mm-style zoom flexibility over maximum reach alone.
- It is preferable to the Canon RF 200-800mm as the default dawn/flight lens because it is easier to acquire birds, zoom out for close passes, and keep moving birds framed.

The best Canon kit is:

1. **Canon RF 24-105mm F4 L IS USM** - wide flock scale, sky, reflections, landscapes, and environmental story frames.
2. **Canon RF 100-500mm F4.5-7.1 L IS USM** - primary wildlife lens for sunrise, sunset, flight, crane pools, ducks, raptors, and general loop work.
3. **Canon RF 200-800mm F6.3-9 IS USM** - distant birds, fields, raptors, and deck work once light is good.
4. **Canon RF 1.4x extender** - optional reach tool for good light. On the RF 200-800mm it is compatible across the zoom range; on the RF 100-500mm it only works from 300-500mm, so it is a more specialized tool and should not be the dawn default.

## Camera-System Decision

### If The Trip Is Sony-Only

- Default Bosque dawn/sunset kit: Sony 24-105mm + Sony 200-600mm.
- Add Sony 100-400mm f/4.5-5.6 when lighter handheld close-pass/flight acquisition matters.
- Add Sony 400-800mm for bright mid-morning deck/field work, distant static birds, and maximum good-light reach.
- Use the 1.4x only after light improves, and mainly for static or distant subjects.

### If The Trip Is Canon-Only

- Default Bosque dawn/sunset kit: Canon 24-105mm + Canon 100-500mm.
- Add Canon 200-800mm only for bright mid-morning deck/field work or distant static birds.
- Use the 1.4x only after light improves, and mainly for static or distant subjects.

### If Both Systems Are Used

- Do not carry Sony 200-600mm, Sony 100-400mm f/4.5-5.6, Sony 400-800mm, Canon 100-500mm, and Canon 200-800mm together unless there is a clear assignment for each body.
- Pick one primary dawn/sunset system and leave the other as backup or midday reach.
- Practical mixed carry: one 24-105, one flexible flight zoom, and optionally one longest zoom in the vehicle for bright field/deck work.

## 24-105mm F4 vs 24-70mm F2.8

Use the **24-105mm f/4** as the Bosque standard zoom.

Why:

- Most wide Bosque frames are fly-outs, sky, water, mountains, weather, habitat, and scale. For those, 70-105mm matters more than f/2.8.
- At dawn, the moving-bird work is happening on the long lens, not at 24-70mm.
- For landscapes and reflections, f/4 is usually enough and we may stop down anyway.
- The 24-105mm range gives better compression and framing for flocks crossing water or mountain bands.

Use a **24-70mm f/2.8** only as a swap if the trip includes people, indoor/event work, night scenes, or a deliberate shallow-depth-of-field look. Do not carry both 24-105mm and 24-70mm for the refuge plan.

## 70-200mm F2.8

Consider a **70-200mm f/2.8** only as an optional fourth lens, not as part of the minimum Bosque wildlife kit.

Where it helps:

- Close cranes/geese near the road, pools, decks, or visitor-center area.
- Compressed environmental frames with birds, water, mountains, cottonwoods, or moon.
- Low-light static subjects when f/2.8 keeps ISO lower than the 100-400/100-500.
- Portraits, people, travel details, lodging/town scenes, and non-refuge photography.
- Subject-isolation frames where shallow depth of field is the point.

Where it does not help enough:

- It is usually too short for primary bird portraits or distant field birds.
- It is not wide enough to replace 24-105mm for blast-offs, big sky, weather, and landscape scale.
- It overlaps the 100-400mm / 100-500mm flight lens enough that carrying both should be intentional.

Recommendation:

- **Sony:** The **Sony FE 70-200mm F2.8 GM OSS II** is a good optional fourth lens if we want f/2.8, portraits, low-light compression, or a teleconverter-compatible mid-telephoto. With the Sony 1.4x it becomes a 98-280mm f/4 lens, but that still does not replace the 200-600mm for primary wildlife reach.
- **Canon:** The compact **Canon RF 70-200mm F2.8 L IS USM** is a good optional fourth lens for travel and portraits. If extender compatibility matters, choose the **Canon RF 70-200mm F2.8 L IS USM Z**, which supports RF 1.4x and 2x extenders through the zoom range.
- If we must stay at **three lenses**, skip the 70-200mm and keep 24-105mm + flight zoom + reach zoom.
- If we allow **four lenses**, the 70-200mm f/2.8 is the fourth lens, not a replacement for the 24-105mm.

## Tanzania Safari Crossover

If this same kit must also work for a Tanzania wildlife safari, the recommendation shifts from **Bosque-optimized bird reach** toward **vehicle-based mammal flexibility**.

Tanzania changes the weighting:

- Large mammals may be much closer than Bosque birds, especially from safari vehicles.
- A 70-200mm f/2.8 becomes much more useful for lions, elephants, giraffes, zebras, people, camp scenes, and low-light mammal portraits.
- A 100-400mm or 100-500mm class zoom becomes the safari backbone because animals can move from close to far while the vehicle position is fixed.
- The 400-800mm / 200-800mm reach lens is still valuable for birds, cats at distance, rhino if the itinerary includes that opportunity, far-off behavior, and compressed landscape wildlife, but it is less automatic than it is for Bosque bird work.
- Dust argues for two bodies and fewer lens changes. Pre-mount the wide/standard lens on one body and the main wildlife zoom on the other.
- Bush flights and small aircraft baggage limits may force the long-reach lens out of the kit.

### Best Shared Sony Kit: Bosque + Tanzania

If we allow four lenses:

1. **Sony FE 24-105mm F4 G OSS** - landscapes, vehicle/camp life, elephants close to the vehicle, environmental wildlife.
2. **Sony FE 70-200mm F2.8 GM OSS II** - close mammals, portraits, low-light compression, cubs/family behavior, camp/travel images.
3. **Sony FE 200-600mm F5.6-6.3 G OSS** - main Tanzania wildlife lens and strong Bosque long bird lens.
4. **Sony FE 100-400mm F4.5-5.6 GM OSS** - lighter Bosque flight and close-pass option.
5. **Sony FE 400-800mm F6.3-8 G OSS** - Bosque reach, Tanzania birds, distant cats, rhino if relevant, and long-compression work.
6. **Sony 1.4x and 2x teleconverters** - useful but avoid frequent mounting/unmounting in dust.

If we must reduce to three lenses for Tanzania travel weight:

- Keep **24-105mm + 70-200mm f/2.8 + 200-600mm**.
- Leave **400-800mm** behind unless birds or distant wildlife are a major priority.
- Bring the **100-400mm** only if close-pass handling and lighter handheld work outweigh the need for one simple long wildlife lens.

### Best Shared Canon Kit: Bosque + Tanzania

If we allow four lenses:

1. **Canon RF 24-105mm F4 L IS USM** - landscapes, vehicle/camp life, close mammals, environmental wildlife.
2. **Canon RF 70-200mm F2.8 L IS USM** or **RF 70-200mm F2.8 L IS USM Z** - close mammals, portraits, low-light compression. Choose the Z version if RF extender compatibility matters.
3. **Canon RF 100-500mm F4.5-7.1 L IS USM** - main Tanzania wildlife lens and Bosque flight/default wildlife lens.
4. **Canon RF 200-800mm F6.3-9 IS USM** - Bosque reach, Tanzania birds, distant cats, rhino if relevant, and long-compression work.
5. **Canon RF 1.4x extender** - useful in good light; remember the RF 100-500mm extender caveat from 300-500mm only.

If we must reduce to three lenses for Tanzania travel weight:

- Keep **24-105mm + 70-200mm f/2.8 + RF 100-500mm**.
- Leave **RF 200-800mm** behind unless birds, distant cats, or very distant wildlife are a priority.
- For Bosque-only bird work, put the RF 200-800mm back in and drop the 70-200mm if needed.

### Bottom Line

For a **Bosque-only kit**, the 70-200mm f/2.8 is optional.

For a **Tanzania safari kit**, the 70-200mm f/2.8 is strongly worth carrying if we allow four lenses. It is the lens that makes the shared kit feel like a safari kit rather than a birding kit.

## Research Synthesis

The clearest pattern across Bosque-specific sources is not "bring the longest lens possible." It is **bring a flexible long zoom first, then add reach when the birds are distant and the light supports it**.

- Cornell's Bosque guide says 300-400mm can be enough for geese and cranes in flight, while 500-600mm becomes heavy enough to benefit from tripod/gimbal support. It also explicitly recommends shorter telephotos and wide lenses for flocks and habitat.
- NANPA recommends a range from 100-400mm upward, with 400mm or larger useful and teleconverters optional.
- WildernessShots recommends at least 500mm and says a 200-600mm zoom is ideal if using one lens.
- Chasing Hippoz recommends 500/600/800mm for isolation, but specifically calls out the Canon RF 100-500mm as a strong Bosque option because of its versatility.
- BirdWatching's Rick Sammon names the Canon RF 100-500mm as his go-to bird/wildlife zoom and shows a Bosque sunrise image made with a 24-105mm lens.
- LifePixel's Bob Coates used a Panasonic 100-400mm Micro Four Thirds setup, which gives an 800mm full-frame equivalent, but the article emphasizes behavior, anticipation, and support as much as reach.
- Thom Hogan's Bosque writeup is a useful counterweight: he made the case that location, timing, moon/subject alignment, and discipline can matter more than simply bringing a 600mm f/4.

## General Recommendations

- Before sunrise: prioritize faster handling and cleaner ISO over maximum reach.
- Avoid teleconverters before sunrise except for static silhouettes.
- Add the 1.4x after light improves for distant static birds, perched raptors, or record shots; reserve the 2x for strong light and supported distant subjects.
- For blast-offs, start wider than expected. The flock shape and sky are often the image.
- For cranes landing or taking off, leave space in the frame for wing spread and direction of motion.
- For Sony flight, prioritize the 200-600mm for reach or the 100-400mm f/4.5-5.6 for lighter close-pass handling. For Canon flight, prioritize the RF 100-500mm.
- For distant groups or perched subjects in good light, use Sony 400-800 or Canon RF 200-800.
- Keep the 24-105 ready for landscapes, flock scale, reflections, habitat, and weather context.
- Carry a 70-200mm f/2.8 only when the fourth-lens slot is worth the extra handling for portraits, close birds, low-light compression, and non-refuge scenes.

## Support And Carry

- Vehicle-based sessions: keep two lenses ready, one long and one wide, with the longest lens stored but accessible if the light improves. A beanbag or window support is more useful than a full tripod inside the auto loop.
- Deck sessions: bring a compact tripod, monopod, or gimbal only if it will not block shared space.
- Highway 1 pullouts: prioritize mobility and safe positioning away from road edges.
- Wind/cold mornings: gloves that allow controls, extra batteries, lens cloth, and stable footing.
- Reflection work: consider lower tripod height only where safe and legal.
- Bring a compatible remote shutter release for tripod work, reflections, landscapes, moon/bird framing, and any slow-shutter flock experiments.
- Do not bring flash for wildlife. It is not useful at Bosque distances, it adds disturbance risk, and refuge rules prohibit using artificial light to spot, locate, or take animals.

### Tripod, Ball Head, And Gimbal Recommendation

Bring support, but do not overbuild the kit.

- **Beanbag / window support:** strongly recommended. Much of Bosque is vehicle-based, and a beanbag is fast, quiet, and low-profile.
- **Tripod legs:** bring a sturdy carbon tripod with no center-column dependence. It should comfortably handle a full-frame body plus a 200-600mm, 400-800mm, or RF 200-800mm class lens.
- **Gimbal head:** recommended if using Sony 400-800mm, Sony 200-600mm, or Canon RF 200-800mm for extended flight sessions. It is the best tool for balanced long-lens panning.
- **Ball head:** useful for 24-105mm landscapes, static reflections, and locked compositions. It is not the ideal primary head for birds in flight with the heaviest lenses.
- **Monopod:** useful compromise on decks and crowded pullouts, especially with the RF 200-800mm or Sony 400-800mm.
- **Remote shutter:** recommended. Use it for tripod-mounted landscapes/reflections, static telephoto compositions, pre-dawn color, and slow-shutter flock blur. It is not needed for hand-held birds in flight.
- **Arca-Swiss lens plates/feet:** standardize all long lenses before travel so switching tripod, gimbal, and monopod support is quick.

### Flash And Artificial Light

Do not include flash in the Bosque wildlife kit.

- Flash will not meaningfully help with cranes/geese at normal refuge distances.
- Flash can disturb wildlife and other photographers.
- The refuge rules prohibit using spotlights, automotive headlights, or other artificial light to spot, locate, or take animals.
- The only reasonable exception would be non-wildlife use away from refuge wildlife, such as a lodging/travel portrait, but it should not be part of the field wildlife kit.

## Positioning Rules

- Birds take off and land into the wind, so wind direction can override the plan.
- Best flight setups happen when wind direction and light direction are compatible.
- Stay in legal pullouts and open areas; no off-road driving or closed-area entry.
- Use the vehicle as a blind when possible.
- Do not approach, flush, feed, or alter wildlife behavior for a shot.
