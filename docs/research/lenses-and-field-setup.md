## General Recommendations

- Before sunrise: prioritize faster handling and cleaner ISO over maximum reach.
- Avoid 1.4x teleconverter before sunrise except for static silhouettes.
- Add the 1.4x after light improves for distant static birds, perched raptors, or record shots.
- For blast-offs, start wider than expected. The flock shape and sky are often the image.
- For cranes landing or taking off, leave space in the frame for wing spread and direction of motion.
- For flight, prioritize the Sony 200-600 or Canon 100-500 over the longest zoom if birds are moving quickly and close.
- For distant groups or perched subjects in good light, use Sony 400-800 or Canon 200-800.
- Keep the 24-105 ready for landscapes, flock scale, reflections, habitat, and weather context.

|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-dawn silhouettes / wide flock | 24-105 + 200-600 | 24-105 + 100-500 | Expose for sky color; compose low horizon and bird shapes. |

## Support And Carry

- Vehicle-based sessions: keep two lenses ready, one long and one wide.
- Deck sessions: tripod or monopod can help, but compact etiquette matters.
- Highway 1 pullouts: prioritize mobility and safe positioning away from road edges.
- Wind/cold mornings: gloves that allow controls, extra batteries, lens cloth, and stable footing.
- Reflection work: consider lower tripod height only where safe and legal.

## Positioning Rules

- Birds take off and land into the wind, so wind direction can override the plan.
- Best flight setups happen when wind direction and light direction are compatible.
- Stay in legal pullouts and open areas; no off-road driving or closed-area entry.
- Use the vehicle as a blind when possible.
- Do not approach, flush, feed, or alter wildlife behavior for a shot.
