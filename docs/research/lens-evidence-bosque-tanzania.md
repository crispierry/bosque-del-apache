# Lens Evidence: Bosque del Apache And Tanzania

## Status

Checked June 6, 2026. This file expands the lens research with two evidence streams:

- Published photographer recommendations for Bosque del Apache and Tanzania safari work.
- Public web photo metadata where focal length, equivalent focal length, camera, or lens details are exposed.

Use this as supporting evidence for `docs/research/lenses-and-field-setup.md`, not as a precise statistical model. Web metadata is incomplete, sometimes inconsistent, and biased toward images whose owners left EXIF or structured data visible.

## How To Read The Evidence

- Article recommendations explain what experienced photographers tell people to pack.
- Metadata examples show what focal lengths were actually used for visible web images.
- Actual focal length and 35mm-equivalent focal length can differ because of crop sensors and compact cameras.
- The conclusion should be practical: choose a kit that covers the repeated evidence bands without forcing too many lens changes in dust, cold, or vehicle conditions.

|---|---|---|
| Cornell Lab / All About Birds, `Bosque del Apache, New Mexico: A Bird Photographer's Playground` | Says 300mm or 400mm can be adequate for geese/cranes in flight, 500mm or 600mm benefits from tripod/gimbal support, and shorter telephotos/wides matter for flock and habitat frames. | Do not define success as "longest possible lens." | Chasing Hippoz, `My Photography & Travel Guide to Bosque Del Apache` | Recommends long telephoto reach for isolation, specifically notes Canon RF 100-500mm versatility, and recommends a second body with 70-200mm or 100-400mm when flocks lift close. | A 100-500mm / 200-600mm class lens is the center; a second body prevents missing wide blast-off frames. |
| The Quite Wild, `Bird Photography for Beginners at Bosque del Apache` | Recommends 100-400mm, 150-600mm, and 200-600mm zooms; lists a telephoto zoom plus 24-105mm as an essential beginner kit. | The evidence supports zoom flexibility over single-purpose primes for most users. |
| LifePixel / Bob Coates, `Bosque del Apache Wildlife Refuge` | Field examples used Panasonic GX85 with 100-400mm, described as an 800mm full-frame equivalent setup, and emphasized behavior, gimbal/handheld handling, and anticipation. | Long equivalent reach helps, but stabilization, tracking practice, and behavior reading are equally important. |
| Luminous Landscape, `Bosque del Apache` | Many examples used Canon 400mm f/4 on a Canon D30 crop body, 70-200mm for environmental flock frames, and 400mm plus 1.4x for distant evening cranes. | Classic Bosque work spans roughly 70mm to 900mm equivalent depending on subject scale. |
| Outdoor Photographer / George Lepp, `Extreme Close-Up` | Bosque snow goose example used Canon 400mm f/2.8 with a 2x extender for 800mm on full frame, with tripod/gimbal support. | Extreme reach is useful for tight flight images, but it is a specialized supported setup, not the only kit logic. |

## Synthesis

### Bosque del Apache

The evidence supports a three-band kit:

1. **Wide/story:** 24-105mm for sky, water, mountains, flock scale, and environmental images.
2. **Flexible long:** 100-400mm / 100-500mm / 150-600mm / 200-600mm for most crane, goose, duck, and raptor work.
3. **Reach specialist:** 400-800mm / 200-800mm / 500mm / 600mm-plus equivalent for distant fields, decks, raptors, and tight bird images after light improves.

Bosque-only priority:

- Sony default: 24-105mm + 200-600mm, with 100-400mm for lighter close-pass flight and 400-800mm only as a good-light reach specialist.
- Canon default: 24-105mm + RF 100-500mm, with RF 200-800mm only as a good-light reach specialist.
- Sigma E-mount alternative: 150-600mm if replacing the Sony 200-600; 60-600mm if one-lens range and dust avoidance matter more than weight; 500mm prime if fixed-framing discipline and a second body are acceptable.

### Tanzania

The evidence shifts the kit toward two-body flexibility:

1. **Close/story/mammal lens:** 24-105mm or 24-70mm, plus 70-200mm f/2.8 if the kit allows it.
2. **Wildlife workhorse:** 100-400mm / 100-500mm / 150-600mm / 200-600mm.
3. **Optional reach:** 400-800mm / 200-800mm / 500mm / 600mm-plus only if birds, distant cats, or specialized wildlife are a major goal and baggage limits allow it.

Tanzania priority:

- Sony: 24-105mm + 70-200mm f/2.8 + 200-600mm is the strongest balanced kit. Add 400-800mm only if birds/distant cats outrank carry weight. Consider Sigma 60-600mm only if one mounted long lens is more important than weight.
- Canon: 24-105mm + RF 70-200mm f/2.8 Z or compact RF 70-200mm + RF 100-500mm is the balanced kit. Add RF 200-800mm only for birds and distant wildlife.
- Pro/rental branch: Sony 300mm f/2.8, Canon RF 100-300mm f/2.8, or Sigma 300-600mm f/4 can improve low-light/action results, but they add cost, weight, and handling demands.
