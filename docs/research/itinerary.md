# Five-Day Photography Itinerary

## Status

Initial photography research captured. Lodging and final date-specific sunrise/sunset calculations still need to be integrated.

## Trip Window Assessment

Early December is a strong photography window for Bosque del Apache.

Why:

- Bosque del Apache's greatest bird numbers are typically early November through late January.
- Winter subjects include sandhill cranes, snow geese, Ross's geese, ducks, raptors, and mammals.
- Early December aligns with the Festival of the Cranes, posted for December 2-5, 2026, which is a strong signal that the timing is prime.

Tradeoff:

- The Festival of the Cranes window will likely bring more photographers and heavier dawn/sunset crowding.
- Roosting and feeding sites change through winter, so same-day visitor center intel is essential.

Date-sensitive baseline, checked June 2, 2026:

- Refuge lands and tour loop: open daily one hour before sunrise to one hour after sunset.
- Visitor center and Nature Store: 9:00 AM to 4:00 PM.
- Daily entrance: $5/private vehicle.
- No camping or overnight parking.
- No drones, off-road driving, closed-area entry, feeding, or wildlife harassment.
- Early December 2026 Socorro sunrise: about 6:55-6:58 AM.
- Early December 2026 Socorro sunset: about 4:57-4:58 PM.
- Sunrise azimuth: about southeast, 116-117 degrees.
- Sunset azimuth: about west-southwest, 243-244 degrees.

## Lens Shorthand

### Sony

- Sony wide: Sony 24-105mm f/4.
- Sony long: Sony 200-600mm.
- Sony reach: Sony 400-800mm.

### Canon

- Canon wide: Canon 24-105mm.
- Canon flex: Canon 100-500mm.
- Canon reach: Canon 200-800mm.

### Teleconverter Rule

Avoid the 1.4x teleconverter before sunrise except for static silhouettes. Add it after light improves, mainly for distant static birds or perched subjects.

## Field Rules Of Thumb

- Check in with the visitor center early because roosting and feeding sites shift through winter.
- Flight Deck/Main Pool can vary with water management.
- Highway 1 pools can be excellent alternatives for sunrise and sunset.
- For flight photography, birds generally take off and land into the wind.
- The best setup is when wind and light come from compatible directions.
