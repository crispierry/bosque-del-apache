## Trip Window Assessment

Early December is a strong photography window for Bosque del Apache.

Why:

- Bosque del Apache's greatest bird numbers are typically early November through late January.
- Winter subjects include sandhill cranes, snow geese, Ross's geese, ducks, raptors, and mammals.
- The November 29-December 5 option overlaps the Festival of the Cranes, posted for December 2-5, 2026, which is a strong signal that the timing is prime.
- The December 6-12 option begins immediately after the festival, so it should still be prime for cranes/geese while likely feeling calmer at dawn and sunset.

Tradeoffs:

- The November 29-December 5 option will likely bring more photographers and heavier dawn/sunset crowding because it overlaps the Festival of the Cranes.
- The December 6-12 option is now the preferred planning bias because it should be easier for lodging and less crowded, but it gives up festival programming.
- Roosting and feeding sites change through winter, so same-day refuge intel is essential.
- Under current visitor center hours, both candidate windows start on Sunday. Visitor Center access should be available Sunday/Monday, closed Tuesday/Wednesday, then available Thursday through the Saturday departure day, unless festival programming or refuge operations alter normal hours.

## Festival of the Cranes Tradeoff

Festival of the Cranes is posted for Wednesday, December 2 through Saturday, December 5, 2026. Current event listings describe it as a major wildlife, birding, and photography festival with roughly 85-90 events/classes over four days, including photography and birding classes, guided tours, hikes, contests, and vendor/community activity.

For this trip, the festival is best treated as a timing signal and an optional experience rather than the default plan:

- Choose November 29-December 5 if the classes, guided outings, expert talks, and community/expo atmosphere matter.
- Choose December 6-12 if the priority is calmer dawn/sunset positions, easier lodging, and a more flexible self-directed photography rhythm.
- Recheck the final festival schedule before booking the earlier window, because registration, event timing, visitor-center activity, and deck/road crowding could affect the best-light photography plan.

## Lens Shorthand

The camera system is intentionally not finalized. Keep both Sony and Canon options in the plan until the final pack list is chosen.

### Sony-Only Default

- Wide/context: Sony 24-105mm f/4.
- Primary wildlife/flight: Sony 100-400mm f/4.5, if available.
- Extra reach in good light: Sony 400-800mm.
- Fallback wildlife zoom: Sony 200-600mm if the 100-400mm f/4.5 or 400-800mm is not available.

### Canon-Only Default

- Wide/context: Canon 24-105mm.
- Primary wildlife/flight: Canon 100-500mm.
- Extra reach in good light: Canon 200-800mm.

### If Both Systems Are Used

Pick one primary dawn/sunset system and avoid duplicate long-zoom carry. A practical mixed plan is either Sony 24-105 + Sony 100-400mm f/4.5, or Canon 24-105 + Canon 100-500, with the longest zoom reserved for bright mid-morning field/deck work.

### Teleconverter Rule

Avoid the 1.4x teleconverter before sunrise except for static silhouettes. Add it after light improves, mainly for distant static birds or perched subjects.

## Field Rules Of Thumb

- Check current refuge information early because roosting and feeding sites shift through winter.
- Flight Deck/Main Pool can vary with water management.
- Highway 1 crane ponds can be excellent alternatives for sunrise and sunset.
- For flight photography, birds generally take off and land into the wind.
- The best setup is when wind and light come from compatible directions.
- If the first two days identify one consistently active roost, repeat it. Bosque rewards responsiveness more than novelty.
