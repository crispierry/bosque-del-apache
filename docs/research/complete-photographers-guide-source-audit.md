## Gaps Found In Existing Sources

- Current conditions: the existing project had strong planning notes, but no authoritative live feed for water levels, closures, burns, road work, or which roosts are active today. The guide should instruct readers to check official refuge updates and ask staff on arrival rather than claiming any deck or pond is currently best.
- eBird access: eBird hotspot pages are essential for scouting but automated access was blocked by a browser challenge. The guide should link to North Loop and South Loop hotspots and explain how to use recent checklists manually.
- Bird behavior: the project had many photographer sources but needed stronger ornithology sources for crane, goose, duck, raptor, and songbird behavior. Cornell species pages fill this.
- Weather and light: the project had a specific December 2026 sun table, but needed general climate and forecast sources for the broader article. NWS and WeatherSpark fill this.
- Ethics: the project already had official FWS rules, but a broader bird-photography ethics source helps explain why no baiting, flushing, crowding, or harassment matters. Audubon fills this, with FWS remaining the legal authority.
- Side trips: Bernardo Wildlife Area was not part of the refuge guide and needs to be clearly separated as an optional complement, not a Bosque location.
- Image licensing: the project already had good FWS public-domain image notes. It still needs per-file checks before adding any non-FWS image.

## Outdated Or Unreliable Source Notes

- Old photographer blogs and classic essays are useful for stable fieldcraft: light, wind, panning, focal length, behavior anticipation, and composition. They should not be used for current hours, fees, road status, closures, active roosts, or lodging.
- Cornell's 2008 Bosque photographer article remains valuable because it is about wind, light, exposure, panning, and the variability of Main Pool. It is not a current access guide.
- Luminous Landscape 2009, LifePixel 2016, Chasing Hippoz 2019, and Thom Hogan's older Bosque writing should be treated as technique and inspiration, not operations.
- NANPA's 2022 event page is a gear/event snapshot. Use its broad lens/support guidance only.
- Lodging prices from June 3, 2026 are planning snapshots, not final booking data. They must be rechecked on direct booking pages for exact dates, room type, tax/fees, cancellation, breakfast, parking, and recent reviews.
- Social/current-sighting sources are useful for "what has been seen recently" but not for legal access or guaranteed photography outcomes.

## Research Standard For The Article

Use official FWS pages for rules, access, fees, roads, restrooms, hours, and refuge operations. Use Friends/Cornell event pages for festival timing and photography community context. Use eBird/Birding Hub/Macaulay as scouting tools, not guarantees. Use Cornell and Audubon for species behavior and ethics. Use photographer reports for technique, lens selection, settings, field strategy, and composition only after cross-checking logistics against official sources.
